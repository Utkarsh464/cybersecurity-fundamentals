## What I Learned

The final module of the final course does what a good close should: it stops teaching _facts_ and teaches **the path forward** — where to keep learning, what communities to join, how to turn everything from the last four courses into momentum that becomes a career.

**Where to keep learning (and proof that learning happened).** The module's through-line is that _documented_ learning compounds: platforms like **TryHackMe** and **Hack The Box** (hands-on practice), **IBM SkillsBuild** (the structured courses I've been through), **Coursera/edX** deeper-dive programs, and **CompTIA Academy / Professor Messer** for Security+ prep. But the key reframe: practice is only as valuable as the **evidence** you produce from it. Writeups, CTF solutions, GitHub repos, lab logs — that is what turns "I took a course" into "I can do the work."

**Communities.** The module emphasizes that cybersecurity is a _community_ field as much as a skills field: **CISA resources**, **NIST frameworks and publications**, **ISACs/ISAOs** (sector-specific threat-information sharing), and the broader practitioner community (meetups, conferences like BSides, Discord/Slack groups, ISC2 and local groups). The network is not a soft extra — it is where the _hidden job market_ lives, and where mentorships that unblock careers actually start.

**Getting started (the honest entry ladder).** The module is refreshingly concrete about the entry path:

- **Start with the baseline + one specialization** (the same "pick a lane" advice from Module 3).
- **Build visible evidence** (this repo IS that evidence).
- **Lead with a portfolio, not a résumé** — hiring managers increasingly verify GitHub/lab work because credentials alone don't prove skill.
- **The IBM Cybersecurity Fundamentals credential (Credly)** is the structured starting point this whole plan has been building toward — it signals foundational breadth, and the work around it (this repo) signals application.
- **Then the certification route**: CompTIA **Security+** as the universal entry credential, then the specialty path (CySA+ or PenTest+ toward SOC/offense, SecurityX/CISSP for senior).

## What Stood Out to Me

The **"portfolio-first"** reframe is the single most actionable idea in the course. It reconciles everything this repo has been doing for months: every writeup, every lab, every link-audit is not just learning — it is a piece of verifiable evidence in a portfolio. The module validated that the exact habit I'd already built (document everything, first-person, with sources) is the very thing employers in this talent-starved market are starved of. I did not need this course to tell me to keep writeups; I needed it to tell me they _count_, and it did.

The **community/hidden-job-market** point is the other one that landed. I had been treating job-hunting as a "post-credential" activity. The module makes the case that the _network_ — ISACs, BSides, Discords, mentorship — is where the referrals happen, and that visibility _while learning_ is what creates the bridges. This repo, published, is part of that presence.
