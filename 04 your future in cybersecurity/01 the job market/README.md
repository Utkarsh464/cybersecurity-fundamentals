## What I Learned

The final course of the plan opens where the previous one ended — with proof that the market is real. But this module adds the numbers I had been missing: where exactly the jobs are, how the demand compares to supply, and why the gap is financially survivable to the employers who experience it. The module is, in essence, the business case for going into this field at all.

**The job market, quantified.** The US Bureau of Labor Statistics projects employment of **information security analysts** to grow **29% from 2024 to 2034** — "much faster than average" — adding roughly **16,000 openings each year**, with a **2024 median annual wage of $124,910**. That is not a niche; it is one of the fastest-growing occupations in the entire US economy (BLS OOH, 2025).

**CyberSeek's live picture.** CyberSeek — the joint NIST NICE + CompTIA + Lightcast initiative — measured **514,359 US cybersecurity job openings over the prior 12 months** (June 2025 data), and computed the **supply/demand ratio at 74%**: the available workforce covers roughly three-quarters of employer demand. That 26% gap is the structural talent shortage.

**The workforce gap, globally.** The module cites the estimate that the global cybersecurity workforce sits near **5 million people** (range 4.4–5.5M across 2022–202 enquiry sources), spanning all industries and regions. The shortfall is not a US-only story.

And the module ties shortage directly to dollars — the **IBM Cost of a Data Breach Report**, which found:

- **53% of breached organizations** were facing **severe security staffing shortages** — up 26.2% year over year.
- Organizations with high-level skills shortages paid an average of **$5.74M per breach vs. $3.98M** for those with low/no shortage — a **$1.76M premium** directly attributable to understaffing.
- **Employee training** emerged as the **#1 cost-reducing factor** in the 2024 report (averaging a **$248 per record** saving vs. the average $176 — wait, the module and the report frame training as the top mitigator overall).

## What Stood Out to Me

The **"74% supply/demand"** framing lands differently than raw job counts. A big number like "500k openings" can feel abstract; a ratio that says "employers can only fill 3 of every 4 demand" is a direct statement about _how hard it is to get hired versus how hard it is to get a job_. For someone building a portfolio, that ratio means the competition is not against a flood of qualified candidates — it is against a _shortage_ of themays, and the differentiator is demonstrable evidence.

The **$1.76M staffing-shortage premium** also reframes "soft skills." It proves organizations are not just short on _technical_ people; they are short on the _workplace_ skills that make teams function — communication, documentation, incident triage under pressure. The most expensive skill shortage in the industry is precisely the skillset this repo is designed to demonstrate.

## Practical Connection

This is the module that flips my existing work from "study" into "career evidence." I don't need a job listing to practice the skills employers are short on — the repo already does that. What this module adds is the strategic framing: the landing page already maps my writeups to the market; I should make sure the repo _itself_ is discoverable and clearly packaged as a portfolio, not just as notes, because that is the exact evidence the gap is starving for.

It also answers a strategic question I had been circling: is a portfolio of personal lab writeups actually worth anything to an employer? The module says _yes_ — because the market is desperate for _demonstrable_ skills, not just credentials. The writeups are the proof.

## Key Takeaways

- **BLS projects 29% job growth** for infosec analysts 2024→2034, ~16k openings/yr, median $124,910 (2024).
- **CyberSeek: 514k US openings, 74% supply/demand ratio** — a real structural talent gap.
- **~5M global cybersecurity workforce**; the shortage is global, not regional.
- **53% of breached orgs had severe staffing shortages**; understaffed breaches cost **$1.76M more** on average.
- **Employee training is the top breach-cost reducer** — workplace skills are the highest-ROI investment.
- The differentiator is **demonstrable evidence**, not credentials alone.
