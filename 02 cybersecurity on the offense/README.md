# Cybersecurity on the Offense

Personal learning notes from the **IBM SkillsBuild: On the Offense** course — course 02 in the Cybersecurity Fundamentals learning plan.

This course sits right after the Introduction to Cybersecurity in the plan: it assumes the CIA triad, controls, risk vocabulary and the legal/ethical boundary built in Course 01, and only then starts teaching how attacks actually work.

## Modules

| #   | Module                                                                  | What is in it                                                                                                                                                            |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1   | [Threat actor groups](<01 threat actor groups/README.md>)               | The threat-actor categories the course covers: script kiddies, hacktivists, criminal gangs, nation-state hackers, malicious insiders and offensive security researchers. |
| 2   | [Types of cyberattacks](<02 types of cyberattacks/README.md>)           | Denial of service, distributed denial of service, phishing, spear phishing, malware, man-in-the-middle, DNS attacks, SQL injection and AI in cyberattacks.               |
| 3   | [Structure of a cyberattack](<03 structure of a cyberattack/README.md>) | How attacks are structured: the Lockheed Martin Cyber Kill Chain, MITRE ATT&CK, and why this matters for defense.                                                        |
| 4   | [Cybercrime ecosystem](<04 cybercrime ecosystem/README.md>)             | How the underground cybercrime economy works: ecosystem, marketplaces, the initial cash injection and the role of cryptocurrency.                                        |
| 5   | [Social engineering](<05 social engineering/README.md>)                 | How attackers manipulate people instead of software: the psychology, the delivery methodsholistic and how to defend against them.                                        |
| 6   | [Open source intelligence](<06 open source intelligence/README.md>)     | Collecting intelligence from public sources: what OSINT is, its sources, the legal boundaries and why it matters.                                                        |
| 7   | [Technical scanning](<07 technical scanning/README.md>)                 | Active reconnaissance: ping, traceroute, port scanning, vulnerability scanning, device search engines and AI-assisted scanning.                                          |
| 8   | [Case studies](<08 case studies/README.md>)                             | Five real incidents (Stuxnet, LAUSD, NSA, Cash App, SolarWinds) read through the frameworks built in Modules 1-4.                                                        |
