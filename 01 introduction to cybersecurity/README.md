# Introduction to Cybersecurity

> **Course:** Introduction to Cybersecurity (IBM SkillsBuild)
> **Part of:** Cybersecurity Fundamentals learning plan — this is the foundational course that builds the shared vocabulary used across the rest of the plan.
> **Status:** 100% COMPLETE

Cybersecurity — IBM writes it as one word — is the practice of protecting people, systems and data from cyberattacks using technologies, processes and policies. IBM frames it in the course not as a purely technical job but as a business enabler: the course repeatedly returns to the ideas that security decisions are business decisionsgele and that everyone in an organization has a role.

This course exists at the bottom of the learning plan's hierarchy. Everything else in the plan — offense, defense, job market — assumes the vocabulary built here: the CIA triad, controls, risk, misconceptions, and the legal/ethical boundaries that separate authorized security work from crime.

## Modules

1. [What is Cybersecurity?](./01%20what%20is%20cybersecurity/README.md) — definition, the CIA triad, controls, and the core threat/hazard vocabulary
2. [Key elements of cybersecurity](./02%20key%20elements%20of%20cybersecurity/README.md) — education, process, and technology as the three pillars
3. [Risk management](./03%20risk%20management/README.md) — risk valuation, risk response, and risk appetite
4. [Common misconceptions about cybersecurity](./04%20common%20misconceptions%20about%20cybersecurity/README.md) — the myths that lead people and businesses to drop their guard
5. [Laws and ethics](./05%20laws%20and%20ethics/README.md) — computer-misuse laws, other notable cybercrime laws, and the ethics discussion

## Why this course matters to the rest of the plan

The legal and ethical module in particular does double duty. It is not only law for its own sake; it draws the line between offensive security research and crime, which is exactly the line that the "On the Offense" course assumes you already understand. The ethics discussion ("what makes using your skills acceptable?") is the same conversation that legitimizes authorized penetration testing as a career — which connects directly to the job-market course later in the plan.
